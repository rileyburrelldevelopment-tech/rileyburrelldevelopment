About the developer

The person building EchoPath.

I’m Riley Burrell, the developer behind EchoPath and Riley Burrell Development. I’m building technology with accessibility, independence and practical everyday use at the centre of the process.

Android closed testing is underway. How to apply for the Android closed beta.

01

My story

Accessibility is personal to the way I think about technology.

My interest in development comes from wanting technology to be more useful, more understandable and more accessible in real situations. EchoPath grew from that same idea: instead of treating accessibility as an extra feature added at the end, I wanted it to influence how the app is structured from the beginning.

That approach shapes the way I think about navigation, spoken information, controls, feedback and the amount of information an app should provide at the right moment.

02

My mission

Build technology that helps people understand and navigate the world around them.

My mission with EchoPath is to create an accessibility-first app that can provide useful navigation, environmental context and visual information without pretending technology can replace a person’s own judgement, mobility skills or other safety tools.

I want the app to be practical, clear and dependable, while keeping its essential accessibility features available to the people who need them.

03

Building EchoPath

It has been built through constant testing and refinement.

EchoPath has grown through repeated development in Xcode, real-device testing and TestFlight feedback. I work feature by feature, test how each change behaves in practice and then refine or rebuild parts that are not reliable or accessible enough.

The project has expanded from core location and navigation features into Apple and Google mapping, travel-mode guidance, Saved Places, safety tools, Scene Detection, Search This Photo and local and cloud photo analysis. I have also started developing the Android version so EchoPath can eventually reach more people across both major mobile platforms.

04

How I work

Real-device behaviour matters more than a successful compile.

Build carefullyFeatures are added in manageable stages rather than rushed into the app.
Test on devicesWhat works in code still has to work properly in somebody’s hand.
Listen to feedbackBeta testing helps identify confusing, unreliable or inaccessible behaviour.
Keep improvingI revisit existing features when there is a better way to make them work.
Protect accessibilityVoiceOver, clear labels, predictable controls and spoken feedback are treated as core behaviour.
Be clear about limitsAI, navigation and visual detection should communicate uncertainty rather than overclaim accuracy.
05

Looking ahead

EchoPath is still growing.

My current focus is preparing EchoPath for its first public iOS release, continuing Android development, improving the website and support information, and continuing to test the app with real users and real journeys.

Riley Burrell Development is also the wider home for the apps, games and ideas I want to build in the future.
